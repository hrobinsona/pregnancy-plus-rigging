## Learning from corrections (Harry: "the workflow should avoid most of these, otherwise there's so much back and forth")

The goal is that a new pose needs no correction round for a mistake Harry has already pointed out once. Three rules:

1. **Pre-flight before building.** Before writing a prompt, a key or a reach, read `docs/HARRY_EYE.md` and write
   `spec.preflight` in the shot: for each item that applies, one line on how this plan avoids it (e.g. "6: both hands
   rest on the bump, none hangs"; "7: the top hand's still asks for the palm on the dome, fingers together, curved toward
   us"; "10: the resting hands get a finger settle at 3.1 s and ride the breath"). A reviewer checks the render against
   that list.
2. **Gate before Harry.** Harry (2026-10-14): "Don't do the pose sheet to me, just iterate with the critic… show me
   once you feel everything is at 8/10." So no intermediate sheets, proofs or renders go to him: the hand-off bar is
   every critic lens at 8/10 or more (fresh reviewers each round), NATURAL: RIGHT and `qa_checks.mjs` PASS. Below the
   bar, iterate with the critics yourself. Nothing goes to Harry until `qa_checks.mjs` passes (or each failure is explained) AND the
   natural lens (`critic_F.py --lens natural`, part of `--lens all`) returns "NATURAL: RIGHT", or each "YES" it lists is
   fixed or explained in the message to him. A "YES" about something the STILL draws differently (a hand, a wrist, a
   pose) is fixed, not explained: "explained" is for points outside the shot's scope. mum3q v7 went to him with "a paw…
   the wrist a ball" open on the top hand; it was a mirrored hand (lesson 53). The natural lens puts each key next to the reference stills it was built
   from (`tools/ref_match.py`) and asks every HARRY_EYE item. The still is the standard for shape, not just position: a
   render can meet the fit's points and still draw a different hand (mum3q v5).
3. **Every note from Harry changes the workflow, not only the shot.** In the same round as the fix:
   - add or sharpen an item in `docs/HARRY_EYE.md` with his words (what he saw, which render);
   - if it can be measured, add a gate (`qa_checks.mjs`, `tip_check.mjs`, …) and check that it FAILS on the render he
     flagged and passes on the fix. A gate that passes the bad render catches nothing (the whole-frame still check passed
     v5's frozen hands; the per-hand `alive` gate fails it);
   - if a reviewer passed what he flagged, sharpen the lens prompt so a fresh reviewer flags it on that render;
   - log the lesson in `docs/PIPELINE.md` and fix the recipe step that let it through (the prompt rules in
     `REF_PROMPTS.md`, the New move steps below).
   A correction that ends as prose only will be made again.

## Non-negotiables

- **Pure code.** Every frame is a pure function of t. No state, no randomness without a seed, no iteration-to-tolerance. `char_anim --verify` checks determinism.
- **References are for measurement only.** Pose-estimate them and fit the rig to them; never trace, composite or draw them into a frame.
- **No linear easing.** Use min-jerk, cubic-bezier eases, Catmull-Rom with time knots, or breath curves.
- **Hands move in hand space** (`spec.reach`): stops, vias and paths. Never blend arm angles between distant poses; that swings the hand on a circle round the shoulder.
- **The client's brief and intensity win.** Put both in `spec.story` and `spec.intensity`; the feel critic sees them. Don't polish an extreme the client didn't ask for.
- **Secrets.** API keys live in `~/.secrets/` or the environment. Never put them in the repo, logs, docs or memory.

## The loop

1. **Brief.** Write the story beat by beat with times, and the intensity in one sentence. Agree it with the client before building.
2. **References: stills, not videos.**
   - Generate one still per key and **two in-betweens per hand move**, using the framing-locked prompts in `docs/REF_PROMPTS.md`. Any image tool works; Codex CLI / ChatGPT images is the user's default.
   - Make them with `tools/refgen.py <char> <name> --prompt-file … --ref sheet --ref prev --ref next -n 3` (Codex CLI on the ChatGPT login; runs on Harry's Mac, not in a cloud container). It registers them through `tools/ref_register.py`. Stills from any other tool: `tools/ref_register.py … --canvas WxH --est`.
   - Use video only to get the TIMING of a fast move: one model, one generation, its own key stills as first and last frame. Take its speed profile, never its path (`PIPELINE.md` §1 scoreboard: 18 of 21 videos were unused).
3. **Fit.** `python3 tools/new_pose.py fit <char> <name>` (or `node tools/fit_pose.mjs <char> refs/pose/<x>.json fit-<name> [--base rest] [flags]`; the base defaults to `bump-rest` if the character has one, else `rest`). Keep the sample with the lowest rms and correct contacts, and record the command in `refs/fit_keys.sh`.
   - If rms > 0.06 H or joints sit on their limits, a DOF is missing. Add it rather than accepting the miss: this is how the head nod (`fx.headPitch`, `engine/warp.js`) and the trunk fold (`--fold` scale DOFs) arrived.
4. **Derive.** Build accents and in-betweens from FITTED poses with `tools/pose_blend.py` and `tools/pose_set.py` (`--add/--set/--fx/--scale/--hand`). Then `tools/plant_feet.mjs`, all in `refs/derive_<shot>.sh`. Re-run the whole script after any change; it is the source of truth for derived poses.
5. **Timeline** (`anims/<shot>.json`):
   - `keys`: body poses with eases. `shift` per channel offsets a key against `spec.lags` for overlap. `only`/`except` restrict a key to some channels, e.g. a fingers-only key.
   - `reach.far`:
     - stops `{t, pose, on}`, plus vias `{via: true}` that are fitted in-betweens;
     - per-stop `limp`/`limpBend`, `skew`, `reg`, `front`;
     - path moves from `tools/vtrack_apply.mjs` (`spec.video`), options documented in `engine/anim.js` pathTarget.
   - `tracks.face`, `fidget` (out-and-back life: fingers, `fx.slide.near.*` strokes, swallows), `breath`, `swaps` (hand views), `pins` (planted near hand), `plantFeet: "ik"`.
6. **Render and measure** (see Commands). Check the per-frame speeds for pops before any review. Use `pose_probe` to A/B a DOF on one frame before keying it, and `uv_check` to see where the wrist and elbow actually go in the torso frame. For a fitted in-between, use its position (`uv_check --poses`) and a path `bias`, not its fitted twist (PIPELINE lesson 21).
7. **QA, then review.** **Every reviewer is Fable** (Harry, 2026-10-15): spawn each critic, natural-lens and blind A/B
   subagent with `model: "fable"` (API id `claude-fable-5-1`). Name the model in the feel log's round entry; if Fable is
   unavailable, say so there instead of silently falling back.
   - `node tools/qa_checks.mjs --char <c> --anim <a> --box …`: frames out of order match, no state leaks, no pixel jumps, no still stretch > 1 s, hands (palm pops, wrist angle one way per move, hands ≥ 15 px apart), alive (each hand moves ≥ 5 px ON her in every 2 s: a hand carried only by her body reads stuck on and lifeless; deliberate holds in `spec.qa.still`), limbs (each 3D arm segment drawn at ≥ 0.85 of its length, lesson 39), fingers (`spec.qa.fingers`, lesson 58). `node tools/qa_closeups.mjs --t …` for joints at size. `node tools/tip_check.mjs --char <c> --anim <a> --from … --to …` while a hand rests on her: how far inside her outline each fingertip ends, and its clearance sideways (reviewers say "tips past the edge" or "a tangent" every round; measure it, lesson 33).
   - Run `python3 tools/critic_F.py <char> <shot> --lens all > out/critic_prompt.md` (feel, Lasseter's principles, hands, natural: the render next to its stills and every `docs/HARRY_EYE.md` item). Give each `out/critic_prompt_<lens>.md` to its own fresh subagent: *"Read … and follow it exactly; open every image it lists; return the review."*
   - For a change: `python3 tools/blind_ab.py <old.mp4> <new.mp4> --anim <c>/<a>`, a fresh subagent on `out/blind_ab/prompt.md`, then `--reveal`. Keep a change that is meant to be visible only if it wins.
   - Give every shot a `handsBox` (film px holding both hands all shot): both packs then show the hands at 1:1 and, per second, what moved (magenta), the upper body too. Stills at phone size hid 20–26 px hand moves: three reviewers in a row called the hands "frozen" while QA measured them moving, and an A/B said it could not see a small change (lesson 58). Before acting on "frozen" or "static", check the motion sheet.
   - Log the round and "What was done" in `characters/<char>/feel_log_<shot>.md`.
   - Run `tools/anim_audit.py` for bleed (clothing showing through limbs and vice versa) and `tools/detail_audit.py` for single poses.
8. **Iterate.**
   - Fix what the client flags first.
   - Anchor every fix on a measurement or a reference.
   - **Explore, don't tune.** If two or three rounds only move a parameter back and forth (the critic oscillates: elbow out = "wing", in = "propped"; wrist 38° = "fist", 18° = "oath"), change the method. Add a fitted in-between still, a DOF or a drawing.

## Engine facts that bite

- **Sign conventions.**
  - Clavicle: + raises near, − raises far.
  - Legs: thigh +a, shin −2a, foot +a keeps the hips over the ankles.
  - Spine: chest + rounds forward.
  - Joint limits silently CLAMP keyed values; check the numbers on screen.
- **Lags and shifts.** `spec.lags` delays channels (`fx.headPitch` can be lagged by its full name). A key's `shift` offsets it per channel.
  - A convulsion leads with the shoulders and head (net 0), hips +1, knees +2.
  - Breaths and sighs are waves.
- **Held hands.**
  - A held contact keeps its fitted wrist bend (`reach.holdReg`).
  - A hand trails its wrist by `handLag × sin(πu)`, so it is never late at the end.
  - The limp wrist follows TIME, so the wrist drops first.
  - Pass `limpBend` to avoid the bend × twist scan, which flips branches.
- **Draw order of the far arm.** It is automatic from the 3D depth proxies. `front` states that a hand is in front; path `behindFrom` states that it is behind.
  - The wrap is continuous (`parts/wrap.js`, `wrapW`); the cuff is cut away round the bump while wrapping.
  - Outline groups are masked to the VISIBLE material, so no line runs over skin that covers it.
- **Elbow.** In path moves, `elbowPath: 'chord'` takes the elbow straight between its two contact positions, so the whole arm lowers.
  - Holding the elbow at a video's position makes the forearm swing round it like a clock hand.
  - With no target, the swivel interpolation can take the elbow out wide.
- **Hands in a turned view.** A hand seen from the back moves in its fan, thumb sweep and slide, not its curl (lesson 46). In ¾ a resting palm keeps its fitted angle (no `lie`); only the wrist goes on the surface.
- **Idle limbs.** A limb the brief doesn't move takes one key's values in every key (`pose_blend --keep`), and a hanging arm counter-rotates the trunk (the far side's sign is opposite), or the fits' noise swings it (lesson 47).
- **Every hand on her is fitted to its still in three steps: SHAPE, then PLACE, then LIFE** (lesson 51; mum3q v6 failed each once), after a step zero.
  **0. Which hand, which face, which arm.** Before shape, read the still's hand by its ANATOMY: which edge the thumb and index are on, where the little finger is, which face we see, where the arm comes from and where its elbow is. The far hand is the OTHER hand: a spec copied from the near one is its mirror image, right only from the palm (hanging), wrong from the back (on the bump): give it `thumbSide` −1 and flip `thumbFx`, and flip any spread keyed raw on it (`hand.far.f*s`; library shapes flip by themselves). `char_check` and `qa_checks handed` gate it (`tools/handed.mjs`). mum3q v1–v7 lay a right hand on her left arm; four rounds tuned its curl, wrist and lines and the critics named only symptoms ("a paw", "a claw", "a thumb pad") until Harry saw "the wrong way round" (lesson 53). When tuning a hand doesn't converge, come back to this step first.
  **1. Shape first, side by side.** `python3 tools/hand_shapes.py <char> <key> --still <still> --side far --region … --shapes a,b --try '[…]'` renders the key with each candidate shape next to the still's hand at the same crop. Match finger LENGTH, curl, how the tips step, the thumb; save the winner in `poses/hands/`. Never pick curl numbers by reasoning: with 3D fingers a hand seen from its back curls INTO the camera, so v6's 70–85° "curled over the top" drew short stacked stubs ("a mitten") and the lower hand's 75–90° "a rake"; the still wanted 45–65°. Grade the curl (index straightest, little finger most curled) so the tips step like the still's instead of ending in a block. Judge the DRAWING in the same sheet, at film size too (`--scale 1`; lesson 52): each finger its own round tip with a small gap beside it (fan the fingers a few degrees), thin lines between them; try drawing settings with `--try '[{"label": "a", "style": {"rim": 0.012, "taper": [1, 0.92, 0.84, 0.76], "tipK": 1.0}}]'` and save the winner in `params.handSpecs`. mum3q v6's thick full-length lines read as "a comb" and "a mitten" through three different curls; its first redraw (thinner lines, pointed tips, fingers together) fused them into "a paddle" at phone size and lost its blind A/B. A sharply bent wrist with a round pad needs its crease (`wristRound.crease`). Pass `--fx '{"contact.near": 1, "contact.far": 1}'` to see the contact shadows as the shot draws them. Eyes are shape too (`--face`): a look-down still draws open eyes under heavy half-lowered lids, the iris low, not slits and not round eyes. Judge them at film size too, and MEASURE the still's eye there: where the white shows (mum3q's look-down: a sliver on the iris's back side only, the iris pushed toward the bump, lids open .75) and how big the dark mass stays. Two guessed whites (white all round; a dark slash) lost blind; the measured one won ("open, plainly looking down at the bump"). An eye pose belongs to its head pose: key the look-down eyes with the head, not 0.6 s before it ("side-eye") (`P.eyeLook`, lesson 57). The head looking down is shape too: the stills TIP it ~20° toward what she looks at and keep the face readable; `fx.headPitch` (a nod warp) past ~15 squashes the face, "bowed too deep" (mum3q v13; n-down now `--add head=8` with headPitch 14; lesson 56).
  **2. Place WITH that shape.** MEASURE the still's hand in its own pixels (zoom it on a 10 px grid; find the lines between the fingers by colour): the wrist, the middle knuckle (where the lines between the fingers start, not the hand's middle) and EACH fingertip, index → little. Run `hand_place.py --keys w,k,t --tips x1,y1,…,x4,y4 --hand <shape> --natural --restarts 40` (the elbow too if it shows). The tips' centroid alone lets the hand roll about it: mum3q v8 met it to 1 px with its tips stacked down one column and its fingers 10° flatter than the still's, "short, nearly horizontal, bunched" (lesson 54). If the tips can't be met (rms over ~5 px), the SHAPE is wrong, not the arm: go back to step 1. Then OVERLAY the placed hand on the still (`mask_overlay.py <char> <key> --still <s> --box …`: red only in the still, blue only in the render): a thumb 8 px short, fat fingers and a fan past the outline looked fine side by side on mum3q v13's lower hand (1682 px of mismatch; 1166 after a slimmer, longer-thumbed hand refitted to its tips; lesson 56). Once the shot runs, overlay the FILM too (`mask_overlay.py <char> <anim>@<t> …`, the shot's pose at t): the reach and the derived trunk had put mum3q's cradling hand 26 px behind its matching key (lesson 58). The tips step like the still's only if the knuckle line slants like the still's (`knuckleArc`, `fingerLen`). A hand lying ON TOP of the bump also takes `--palm-down 0.55`: facing away from the camera, its curl only shortens the fingers; palm down onto the dome, the same curl bends them down over it on screen. Re-run `hand_shapes.py` on the placed key: shape and place are one loop, usually two turns:
  - a VISIBLE arm: `--hide none --elbow ex,ey` (q_k4_down's near hand sat 50 px low after the joint fit: the drawn forearm is shorter than the illustration's);
  - an arm the still shows only past her outline: first say WHICH segment that strip is. The far arm coming down past her bust to a hand on top of the bump is the hanging UPPER arm, its elbow at her side on the bump's edge and the forearm pointing at us over the top (the still's round mass): `--hide none --elbow ex,ey --hang 0.8`, the elbow under that mass. mum3q v7 took the strip for the forearm (`--fore` on it): the solve lifted the elbow back to her bust, "a chicken wing" whose forearm end was a ball over the bump (lesson 53). `--fore fx,fy` is for a forearm the still really shows;
  - a HIDDEN wrist (the far hand on top of the bump, its arm behind her): the default `--hide arm`, `--slack 15 --restarts 60`, the knuckle left out when the palm curves toward us (MediaPipe puts a hidden wrist at the fingertips; without `--natural` the best fit can be an arm through her chest). A hand whose arm is hidden can read stuck on (HARRY_EYE 9): show the arm when the still shows it;
  - a hand RESTING on her: `--hand-len 0.85`. Illustrations draw it nearly unforeshortened; solved pointing at the camera (68%) it read as a stubby mitten even with its points met;
  - a hand DRAPED over the dome (the back of the hand on its top, the fingers down its curve, as q_k4_down_0): it bends at the KNUCKLES (MCP 45–55°, PIP 8–14°, graded mildly), the fingers straight; a curl in the finger joints draws "a paw". Its little-finger side wraps down and away, so that hand's spec takes a knuckle arc and finger lengths that step the tips (`knuckleArc` [1, 1, .95, .85], `fingerLen` [1, 1, .9, .74] on mum3q's far hand), and `lineMcpK` 0.25 so the lines between the fingers stay whole through the drape (only a PIP folds a finger over its own line; lesson 54);
  - then judge the SHAPE on `ref_match.py`'s sheet (finger direction and curl, the wrist, the heel), not the rms. 3D fingers (`flexFx: { from: 'hand.<side>' }` in the hand spec) curl in the hand's own plane, toward the palm: on a palm-down hand a curl graded from index to little finger swings the more curled fingers sideways and opens gaps, so a hand lying together takes a mild grade and a spread closed toward the middle finger (mum3q hold). A forearm pointing at the camera is drawn END-ON (`parts/arms.js`: a round mass, its tube thinned inside it; `P.armFar.endOn` shapes the mass, picked beside the still: mum3q's lies along the upper arm, `axis: 'upper'`, flowing out of its outer edge, where an ellipse along the short forearm read as "a knob"; a shadow group's `noCast` keeps a mass standing off her in the air from casting, lesson 54). Read the still's OVERLAPS before its outline: mum3q's end-on wrist lies OVER the hand's heel (`endOn.front`) and BEHIND the bump (`endOn.cut: 'dress'`), with one short arc where the hand folds into it (`endOn.lines`) and no outline round it; v11 matched the still's silhouette to the pixel with the order wrong and read as "a knob ringed by a crease" (lesson 55); a sharply bent wrist on a forearm across the picture can take a heel pad (`P.arm/armFar.wristRound`), never wider than its limb. Where a hand lies over its own arm, an outline group draws its edge there (`C.outlineGroups`), as the stills do.
  Pin a held hand with a reach and `keepSize: true` (or the arm's 3D solve makes it grow and shrink as her body moves); over a forearm seen END-ON add `keepPalm: true` (the breath turned mum3q's end-on forearm 10° and flattened the draped fingers 5°; lesson 54). Then MEASURE the hand's finger angles on the film's frames against the still's, not only on the key, and gate them: `spec.qa.fingers` [{side, t, want, tol}] (the derived look-down trunk turned mum3q's pinned cradling hand 6° steeper than its fitted key, "a comb"; set the hand bone of the reach stop's pose in the derive script until it matches, lesson 58), and gate a resting hand ALL SHOT, not only at its keys: `every: {step: 0.5, tol}` and `fan: {every: 0.5, tol}` (the fan's shape: each finger minus the four's mean, so a whole-hand turn passes); a fidget between the keys made mum3q's cradling fingers "a rake" while the key frames passed (lesson 59); if the arm's elbow wanders or hops under the pinned hand (the `qa_checks` elbow gate), add `keyedArm: true` to its stops: the arm stays the fitted one and only a damped solve moves the wrist. A stroke on the surface it rests on marks its stops `slide: true`; declare a hidden or designed-short segment in `spec.qa.limbs` (lessons 48–49, 51).
  **3. Life a viewer sees on a phone.** A resting hand caresses, presses and strokes in moves of 10–20 px, timed to her face (a caress while she looks down, a squeeze as she smiles, a stroke through the smile, a settle as her eyes come back). A reach's `dp` is in H (mum3q: 1 H ≈ 225 px; v6's 0.02 H "lift" was 4.5 px and both reviewers saw frozen hands). A DRAPED hand (bent at its knuckles over the dome) squeezes and presses at its knuckles, the finger joints straightening: curling its finger joints bunched mum3q's into "a paw" (lesson 54). A hand seen PALM-ON (cradling from below) is the opposite: a knuckle bend turns its fingers toward the camera, so they flatten and lengthen into "a rake"; gather it at the END joints (graded, index least) with the whole hand turned a few degrees to cup (lesson 59). Raw finger spreads (`f*s`) in a fidget skip `spreadSign`, so on a mirrored hand they open the other way: check the fan on the film before flipping them (v23's flip read "bunched"). Slide the wrist little and turn the hand about its wrist with a `hand.<side>` fidget (it lands after the reach, so the arm stays put): a 14–18 px wrist slide pulled the far forearm in behind her bust (the shrinking arm), and a `foreRot` twist opened a white gap under her bust. A hand lying palm down is seen nearly edge-on, so its turn barely shows (mum3q v7: 24° moved the tips ~6 px): move its fingers instead (lift and settle, a squeeze), and measure every gesture in the rendered frames, not by its degrees. A digit moving ALONE reads as a tic: mum3q's cradling thumb swinging out by 26° while the rest of the hand held read "a thumb tic, not a resting hand that shifts a little" (blind A/B v17): turn the whole hand a few degrees with it and halve the digit. Cross the loop's seam with a small gesture. A turn about the wrist changes how foreshortened the hand is: keep it inside the limbs gate's 10% (mum3q v8: 24° on the end-on arm grew the hand 12%; 14–17°). Gate: `qa_checks` alive, 5 px on her in every 2 s (lessons 52, 53).
- **A breakdown is half way in what moves**, whatever its still shows: q_k_mid_2 was 85% up, so the look up did its travel in 0.3 s (lesson 51). Hold the key back in the derive script (`pose_blend --add`).
- **When the stills disagree on something the client will see** (which hand is on top), put the choice to them on a decision card with the samples, and keep working on the recommended one: Harry picked "keep far on top" in one tap, which saved a round (lesson 51).
- **Ask the stills for the pose a body makes** ("the palm on the dome, fingers together, gently curved toward us"), never for a drape over an edge: a reference that says "draping over the edge" gives fingers hanging off it, which no one does (Harry on mum3q v4, lesson 49).
- **Measured motion.** `uv_check` (torso frame), `--tracks-only` (speeds) and `tools/anim_dump.mjs` (channels, head and hip positions) settle arguments: "lunge vs fold", "the head only drops".
- **A cut of a shot for another film** (a window of a loop, a face swapped): copy the spec, set `duration` and `loop: false`, keep only the keys, tracks, events and fidgets inside the window, and pin `drift.freqs` to the loop's harmonics (`k / D`: a non-loop shot drifts at its own frequencies). Prove it with `node tools/anim_compare.mjs <char> <source> <cut>` (mum3 `breathe-open-neutral`: 0.03 px).
- **Eyes in ¾ point by the iris's place in the eye.** A centred iris looks at the camera, whatever the lids do: mum3q's look-down "eyes first" with a centred iris read "looking at us from under heavy lids… coy" (lesson 59). Move the lids and the iris together toward what she looks at.
- **A weight shift moves the hips, not the feet.** Tilt both thighs together from the ankles (plantFeet `ik`) and set `spec.plantFeetSink: true`: a straight leg can't stretch, so without it the feet lift 3 px at the far end of the sway (mum3 ease-sway, lesson 60). Check with `node tools/feet_probe.mjs <char> <anim>` (feet drift ≤ 1 px). Keep the head steady in space (measure it with `anim_dump`): leaning the trunk back to "trail" the hips bobbed mum3's head and lost blind; one sway, its settle far smaller than the move. A knee bends with a NEGATIVE shin angle (read the bone's `min`/`max`; mum3's `shin.near=+3` was a clamped hyperextension). At phone size a smile's lid squint reads as closed eyes: a look at something keeps the iris showing (lesson 60).
- **Check a bone's sign before claiming a direction.** `node tools/bone_signs.mjs <char> <pose> --bones clavicle` prints which way +deg moves each bone's end on screen. Signs differ per bone and per side: + LOWERS mum3q's far clavicle, so n-up's "shoulders up" `clavicle.far=2` dropped her far shoulder for ten versions (lesson 61), as `shin=+3` was a hyperextension (lesson 60).
- **Measure an outline a reviewer names by its EDGE, row by row, and through the whole shot.** `mask_overlay.py <char> <anim>@<t> --still <s> --box … --edge right` prints the outer edge every 10 px against the still's. mum3q's far arm ("a mitten… a club") overlaid as ~2200 px either side; its edge showed the arm 8° too upright, a hump at the shoulder (+17 px) and a corner into the wrist (−11 px). Fitting that edge at the still's one frame (the shoulder lower and in, the arm widening to the wrist: 6.5 → 1.1 px rms) LOST blind (v27): through the look up the lower shoulder hid the arm above her bust, "the arm only appears below the bust… a wedge", where v26's small shoulder cap let the eye follow "shoulder to arm to hand". Run the edge at every key of the shot, not only the still's frame: a form fitted at one frame can read worse in the moves; and keep a limb's path from the body readable (lesson 61).

## New character checklist

Worked through end to end with mum3 (2026-10-12; PIPELINE lessons 26–32). Template: mum3 (profile, bare arms); mum2 only for a ¾ figure in a cardigan.

1. **Design the master for the rig** (REF_PROMPTS "Design rules"): bare arms or fitted short sleeves, near profile or a ¾ view whose moves cross the picture plane, the far arm hidden or simple, top and bottoms in different values, plain white background, full body. A sleeve or cuff is a separate piece pivoting on a pin: that is where mum2's bleed and "pinned on" look came from. Generate candidates through the worker (`requests/refgen/<name>.json`, 1–3 refs) and let the client pick.
2. **Scaffold:** `python3 tools/new_char.py <id> --master <png>` copies mum3's code, hand library and base poses and prints the steps.
3. **Measure:** `python3 tools/master_measure.py <id> characters/<id>/ref/master.png` → `ref/landmarks.json` + `ref/measure.png`. LOOK at every point on the overlay; a wrong point is a wrong drawing (lesson 27). Calibrate the fitter: `python3 tools/pose_est.py characters/<id>/ref/master.png --out characters/<id>/refs/pose/master.json`.
4. **Draw from the measurements:** `params.js` (joints, named points, widths, hand spec) and `parts/*.js`. Each limb is ONE tube built in posed space (a filleted elbow, no sleeve over a joint); clothing that crosses a joint is skinned to both bones or built on the limb's own contour (mum3's strap). Gate: `char_check`, `char_compare.py <id> rest` IoU ≥ 0.95, and look at the compare image row by row.
5. **Declare what the design hides:** `fit: { hidden: ['far.arm'] }` (skipped by the fit, not drawn, skipped by QA and the critic's hand pages). Gate the contact shadow by a contact track (`groups: [{match, gate: 'contact.near'}]`) so a hanging hand casts none.
6. **Hands early:** `python3 tools/hand_fit.py <id>` against the master (centre the hand on the wrist's WIDTH, not the estimator's wrist point; mum3's sign error left a shelf). The far hand is the other hand: mirror its spec for the face the shots show (from the back: `thumbSide` −1, `thumbFx` flipped), and declare in `C.qa.handed` any view it can't draw yet; `char_check` gates it (lesson 53). Then pick the hand's DRAWING beside the stills' hands, zoomed and at film size (`hand_shapes.py --try '[{"style": {...}}]' --scale 1`: line weight `rim`, `taper`, `tipK`, `fingerW`, the thumb's `w`): copied from mum3, mum3q drew thick full-length finger lines for six versions, "a comb", "a mitten"; thinner lines with pointed tips then fused the fingers into "a paddle" (lesson 52). Add view shapes to `poses/hands/` as the shots need them (mum3: `lay`, fingers curved onto a surface).
7. **Key stills → fits → a simple first shot** (breathing, a hand to the bump) through the loop above, then the three-lens review. Fix the skill wherever it gets stuck, and log each lesson in `docs/PIPELINE.md`.
